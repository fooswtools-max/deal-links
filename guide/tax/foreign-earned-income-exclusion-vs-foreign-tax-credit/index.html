What is the foreign earned income exclusion?

The foreign earned income exclusion (FEIE) allows U.S. taxpayers living abroad to exclude up to $120,000 (2023 limit) of their foreign-earned income from U.S. federal income tax. To qualify, taxpayers must meet either the bona fide residence test or the physical presence test, which require living outside the U.S. for a specific period. The FEIE applies only to earned income, such as wages or self-employment income, and does not cover passive income like dividends or rental income. This exclusion is claimed using Form 2555 and can significantly reduce taxable income for expatriates. However, it does not eliminate the obligation to file a U.S. tax return.

What is the foreign tax credit?

The foreign tax credit (FTC) allows U.S. taxpayers to offset U.S. income tax liability for taxes paid to a foreign government on foreign-sourced income. This credit prevents double taxation and applies to both earned and passive income. Taxpayers can claim the FTC using Form 1116, and the credit is limited to the lesser of the foreign taxes paid or the U.S. tax attributable to the foreign income. For example, if a taxpayer paid $5,000 in foreign taxes and the U.S. tax on that income is $4,500, the maximum credit is $4,500. The FTC is generally more beneficial than the foreign earned income exclusion for taxpayers in high-tax countries.

How do I qualify for the foreign earned income exclusion?

To qualify for the foreign earned income exclusion (FEIE), taxpayers must meet either the bona fide residence test or the physical presence test. The bona fide residence test requires living in a foreign country for an uninterrupted period that includes an entire tax year. The physical presence test requires being physically present in a foreign country for at least 330 full days during a 12-month period. For example, if you move abroad on January 1, 2023, you must remain abroad until at least December 1, 2023, to meet the 330-day requirement. Both tests exclude short trips to the U.S. and require maintaining a tax home in a foreign country.

How do I claim the foreign tax credit?

To claim the foreign tax credit (FTC), U.S. taxpayers must file Form 1116 with their federal tax return. This form calculates the credit based on foreign taxes paid on income sourced outside the U.S. The FTC is limited to the lesser of the foreign taxes paid or the U.S. tax attributable to the foreign income. For example, if you paid $3,000 in foreign taxes and the U.S. tax on that income is $2,500, your credit is limited to $2,500. Taxpayers must provide documentation of foreign taxes paid, such as tax receipts or withholding statements. The FTC can be carried back one year or forward up to 10 years if unused.

What are the income limits for the foreign earned income exclusion?

The foreign earned income exclusion (FEIE) allows U.S. taxpayers to exclude up to $120,000 (2023 limit) of foreign-earned income from U.S. federal income tax. This limit is adjusted annually for inflation. For example, in 2022, the limit was $112,000. The exclusion applies only to earned income, such as wages or self-employment income, and does not cover passive income like dividends or rental income. Married couples filing jointly can each claim the exclusion if both meet the eligibility requirements, effectively doubling the limit to $240,000. However, unused portions of the exclusion cannot be transferred between spouses.

Can I use both the foreign earned income exclusion and the foreign tax credit?

Yes, taxpayers can use both the foreign earned income exclusion (FEIE) and the foreign tax credit (FTC), but not on the same income. The FEIE excludes up to $120,000 (2023 limit) of foreign-earned income from U.S. tax, while the FTC offsets U.S. tax liability for taxes paid on foreign income not excluded by the FEIE. For example, if you earn $150,000 abroad and exclude $120,000 under the FEIE, you can claim the FTC for foreign taxes paid on the remaining $30,000. However, using the FEIE reduces the amount of foreign income eligible for the FTC, potentially lowering the credit’s benefit.

What are the pitfalls of the foreign earned income exclusion?

One pitfall of the foreign earned income exclusion (FEIE) is that it does not exempt taxpayers from paying Social Security or Medicare taxes on excluded income. Additionally, the FEIE applies only to earned income, leaving passive income like dividends or rental income fully taxable. Taxpayers must also meet strict eligibility tests, such as the bona fide residence or physical presence test, which require significant time abroad. For example, failing to meet the 330-day physical presence test disqualifies you from the exclusion for that year. Lastly, using the FEIE reduces the amount of foreign income eligible for the foreign tax credit, potentially increasing overall tax liability.

What are the alternatives to the foreign earned income exclusion?

Alternatives to the foreign earned income exclusion (FEIE) include the foreign tax credit (FTC) and the foreign housing exclusion or deduction. The FTC allows taxpayers to offset U.S. tax liability for taxes paid to foreign governments, making it more beneficial in high-tax countries. The foreign housing exclusion or deduction allows taxpayers to exclude or deduct certain housing expenses incurred abroad, up to a limit based on the FEIE amount. For example, in 2023, the housing deduction limit is $16,656. Taxpayers can also choose to forego the FEIE entirely and rely solely on the FTC, depending on their specific tax situation and foreign income sources.

How does the foreign tax credit limit work?

The foreign tax credit (FTC) limit ensures taxpayers cannot claim more credit than the U.S. tax attributable to their foreign income. The limit is calculated using Form 1116 and equals the lesser of the foreign taxes paid or the U.S. tax on the foreign income. For example, if you paid $4,000 in foreign taxes and the U.S. tax on that income is $3,500, your FTC is limited to $3,500. The limit applies separately to different categories of income, such as passive or general income. Any unused credit can be carried back one year or forward up to 10 years. This mechanism prevents taxpayers from reducing their U.S. tax liability below zero using the FTC.

What is the foreign housing exclusion?

The foreign housing exclusion allows U.S. taxpayers living abroad to exclude certain housing expenses from taxable income. This exclusion is calculated based on the foreign earned income exclusion (FEIE) limit and varies by location. For example, in 2023, the base housing amount is $16,656, and taxpayers can exclude additional amounts depending on their city of residence. Eligible expenses include rent, utilities, and repairs, but not mortgage payments or capital improvements. Taxpayers must meet the same eligibility requirements as the FEIE, such as the bona fide residence or physical presence test. The exclusion is claimed using Form 2555 and can significantly reduce taxable income for expatriates.
