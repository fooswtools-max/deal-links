What is the SAVE plan for student loans?

The SAVE plan is an income-driven repayment (IDR) program designed to lower monthly student loan payments based on income and family size. It replaces the Revised Pay As You Earn (REPAYE) plan and offers more favorable terms, such as excluding a portion of income from the payment calculation. For example, borrowers earning less than $32,805 annually ($15/hour full-time) may qualify for $0 monthly payments. The plan also prevents interest from accruing beyond the monthly payment amount, reducing the overall loan balance over time. To qualify, borrowers must have federal student loans and demonstrate financial need. Applications are processed through the U.S. Department of Education or loan servicers.

How do I apply for the SAVE plan?

To apply for the SAVE plan, borrowers must complete the Income-Driven Repayment Plan Request form on the Federal Student Aid website or through their loan servicer. The application requires details about income, family size, and tax filing status. Borrowers can link their IRS data for automatic income verification or submit alternative documentation, such as pay stubs or a tax return. Processing typically takes 2–4 weeks. Once approved, payments are recalculated based on the borrower’s income and family size. For example, a single borrower earning $40,000 annually may see monthly payments drop to around $150. Borrowers must recertify their income annually to remain on the plan.

What happens to my SAVE plan after it ends?

When the SAVE plan ends, borrowers revert to their previous repayment plan, typically the Standard Repayment Plan, unless they choose another option. Monthly payments may increase significantly, as the Standard Plan aims to repay the loan in 10 years. For example, a $30,000 loan at 5% interest would result in payments of approximately $318 monthly. Borrowers can reapply for SAVE or switch to another IDR plan if they still qualify based on income. Failure to act may lead to default, impacting credit scores and eligibility for future financial aid. Borrowers should contact their loan servicer for guidance before the plan ends.

What are the eligibility requirements for the SAVE plan?

Eligibility for the SAVE plan requires having federal student loans, including Direct Loans and FFEL Program loans, and demonstrating financial need based on income and family size. Borrowers with Parent PLUS loans are ineligible. For example, a borrower earning $50,000 annually with a family of four may qualify for reduced payments. Borrowers must also be in good standing, meaning they are not in default. Those with defaulted loans must rehabilitate them before applying. Income verification is mandatory, and borrowers must recertify annually. Applications are submitted through the Federal Student Aid website or loan servicers, and approval depends on meeting all criteria.

How does the SAVE plan calculate monthly payments?

The SAVE plan calculates monthly payments based on discretionary income, which is the difference between adjusted gross income (AGI) and 225% of the federal poverty guideline for the borrower’s family size. For example, a single borrower earning $40,000 annually with no dependents would have discretionary income calculated as $40,000 minus $32,805 (225% of the 2023 poverty guideline), resulting in $7,195. Payments are 10% of discretionary income divided by 12, or about $60 monthly. Borrowers earning less than $32,805 may qualify for $0 payments. The plan also prevents interest from exceeding the monthly payment amount, reducing overall loan costs.

What are the benefits of the SAVE plan compared to other IDR plans?

The SAVE plan offers several advantages over other IDR plans, including lower monthly payments and interest subsidies. For example, payments are calculated at 10% of discretionary income compared to 15% under the Income-Based Repayment (IBR) plan. Additionally, SAVE excludes a larger portion of income from the calculation, reducing payments further. Borrowers earning less than $32,805 annually may qualify for $0 payments. The plan also prevents interest from accruing beyond the monthly payment amount, unlike other IDR plans. This makes SAVE particularly beneficial for borrowers with low incomes or high loan balances. However, eligibility and benefits vary based on individual circumstances.

What happens if I miss a payment on the SAVE plan?

Missing a payment on the SAVE plan can lead to late fees and potential delinquency, but borrowers have options to avoid default. Late payments incur a fee of up to 6% of the missed amount. For example, a missed $100 payment could result in a $6 fee. Borrowers can request forbearance or deferment to temporarily pause payments if facing financial hardship. However, interest may continue to accrue during forbearance, increasing the loan balance. Borrowers should contact their loan servicer immediately to discuss alternatives. Persistent missed payments can lead to default, which damages credit scores and triggers wage garnishment or tax refund offsets.

Are there alternatives to the SAVE plan for student loan repayment?

Yes, alternatives to the SAVE plan include other income-driven repayment (IDR) plans, such as Income-Based Repayment (IBR), Pay As You Earn (PAYE), and Income-Contingent Repayment (ICR). Each plan calculates payments differently, with IBR and PAYE capping payments at 10% or 15% of discretionary income. For example, a borrower earning $50,000 annually may pay $250 monthly under IBR compared to $200 under SAVE. Borrowers can also opt for the Standard Repayment Plan, which aims to repay loans in 10 years, or extended repayment plans lasting up to 25 years. Private refinancing is another option but forfeits federal loan benefits. Borrowers should compare plans based on their financial situation.

How does the SAVE plan affect loan forgiveness programs?

The SAVE plan qualifies borrowers for Public Service Loan Forgiveness (PSLF) and other forgiveness programs after making 120 qualifying payments. For example, a borrower working full-time for a government agency or nonprofit may have their remaining balance forgiven after 10 years. Payments made under SAVE count toward forgiveness, even if they are $0 due to low income. Additionally, SAVE offers forgiveness for undergraduate loans after 20 years and graduate loans after 25 years of repayment. Borrowers must recertify their income annually and ensure their employment qualifies for PSLF. Forgiveness amounts are tax-free under current federal law.
